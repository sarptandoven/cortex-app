Privacy

Cortex privacy

Draft — pending legal review before public launch. This page is written to be accurate to how the software actually behaves; it is not yet a lawyer-reviewed policy.

Cortex has two ways to run, and your data is handled differently in each. The local-first macOS app keeps your memory on your own Mac and needs no account. Cortex Cloud is an optional hosted tier you sign in to; it exists to sync and serve your memory to AI tools, and is described honestly below.

Local app — the default

  • Memory lives on your Mac at ~/Library/Application Support/Cortex/Cortex.vault/ (or a folder you choose).
  • The local app requires no account and no cloud service. It works with the network off.
  • Cortex reads clipboard content only when you trigger a capture, and a local notes folder only after you choose it.
  • It does not record your screen, listen through the microphone, or capture ambient activity.
  • Stored: memories you approve, plus extracted tasks, entities, topics, graph edges, settings, audit events, and local backups — all as readable files plus a rebuildable index.

Cortex Cloud — optional hosted accounts

  • You create an account with an email + password, or by signing in with Google or GitHub. That account is how we identify you; we store your email, login identifiers, and authentication records.
  • Your data is stored in an isolated per-tenant store on our server so one account cannot reach another's.
  • Encryption, stated plainly: your connector credentials (the tokens Cortex uses to read your connected sources) are encrypted at rest with a key unique to your account; broader at-rest encryption of memory content is rolling out. This is encryption at rest with a per-user key — it is not end-to-end or "zero-knowledge": Cortex servers can read your memory in order to build embeddings, retrieve, and serve cited context to your AI tools.
  • Deletion: deleting your account destroys your per-user key, which permanently renders your encrypted credentials — including in any backup — unreadable, and removes your derived data. As broader content encryption ships, this crypto-erasure extends to it.
  • You can export your memory and revoke any connected tool or token at any time.

What Cortex reads from connected sources

  • Connectors are read-only and only run for sources you explicitly connect (e.g. Obsidian, Gmail, GitHub, Slack, Notion, Calendar).
  • Cortex does not crawl accounts you have not connected, and does not read browser history or Messages unless you connect a source that provides them.

AI tool sharing

  • Context is shared with an AI tool only when you connect it (a scoped token) and only within the permissions you grant.
  • New sources start with review enabled, reads enabled for retrieval, redaction enabled, and tool saves routed into Review.
  • Exports, maintenance, and destructive actions are off until you opt in.
  • Redaction masks common tokens, secrets, emails, and long account-like numbers before context is shared.
  • Support bundles omit raw capture text, memory bodies, copied chat context, and exported data.

Your rights

  • Access and export: request or download everything associated with your account.
  • Deletion: delete your account and data (see crypto-erasure above).
  • Questions or requests: contact us below. Do not send raw notes, chats, memory records, prompts, exports, API keys, or tokens by email.

For Limitless & Rewind users

Your memory outlived the company that stored it.

Rewind became Limitless. Then Meta acquired the team, and the personal, local-first memory a lot of people built their days around is winding down. If you exported your lifelogs before the lights went out — or you're doing it right now — Cortex is a place to keep them.

Cortex is a private, owned home for that history. Your export imports in one step, and it stays on your Mac. No account is required to import, and nothing is uploaded to us to do it. This time the vault is yours.

What Cortex does with your Limitless export

  • Reads your lifelogs. Cortex understands the Limitless lifelogs export — the lifelogs.json / .jsonl the app and the Developer API produce — as well as markdown or text transcript dumps (including the older Rewind ones). A .zip of any of those works too.
  • Keeps each day as a memory. Every lifelog becomes a searchable record: the speaker-labelled transcript, a clear title, and the date it happened. Long days are split into sensible pieces so they stay useful instead of one giant blob.
  • Preserves who said what and when. Speaker names and timestamps carry over, so a memory still knows it was you at 9:05 on a Tuesday.

Import it in one step

  1. Export from Limitless Download your lifelogs from Limitless (or use an export you already have — a lifelogs.json, a .jsonl, a folder of markdown/text transcripts, or a .zip of them). A legacy Rewind transcript export works too.
  2. Open Cortex Download the macOS app and open it. You don't need an account to import.
  3. Drop in the export Point Cortex at the exported file, folder, or .zip. Cortex recognizes a Limitless / Rewind export on its own and shows you how many lifelogs it found before anything is saved.
  4. Review and keep Your days land as memories you can review, search, and cite. From here they're part of one vault you own — alongside anything else you connect.

The privacy posture, plainly

  • Local-first. The import runs entirely on your Mac. Your export is read from disk and written to a local vault. It works with the network off.
  • You own the vault. Your memories are readable records on your own disk with a rebuildable search index next to them — not locked inside a service you can't leave. You can back them up, move them, or delete them yourself.
  • No account needed to import, nothing uploaded to us. Importing your Limitless history requires no sign-up, and Cortex doesn't send your export anywhere to do it.
  • Optional cloud is separate and honest about itself. Cortex Cloud (multi-device sync and hosted retrieval) is an opt-in you turn on later; it is encrypted at rest but is not yet end-to-end encrypted, and the Privacy page says so plainly. Import doesn't touch it.

Honest limits

  • Cortex imports the data in your export — transcripts, titles, timestamps, speakers. It does not import Limitless's app UI, audio recordings, or any feature that only ran inside their service.
  • Export formats change. Cortex is built to be tolerant — a malformed entry is skipped, not fatal — but if your export looks different from what we handle, tell us and we'll add it. Email support@trydoppl.com.
  • Cortex is an early macOS beta. We'd rather under-promise here than repeat the thing that just happened to you.
